System architecture

Not one net. The right structure for the threat.

Bird netting, hail protection, rain film, insect exclusion and shade all behave differently. The right design depends on the crop, wind exposure, soil, region and the grower’s operating budget.

Netting guide

Core cover types and where they fit.

The tighter or more solid the cover, the higher the wind load. This is why bird netting can use wider spans, while rain film needs smaller bays, stronger hold-downs, or a release system.

Cover type Typical use Common sizing / format Protects from Design notes
Bird net General orchard bird and bat exclusion Often 12–20mm mesh; quad, diamond or hex patterns Birds, bats, some wind/sun reduction Good airflow. Suitable for wider post spacing. Must be wildlife-safe and properly tensioned.
Hail net Apples, pears, stone fruit, cherries and citrus UV-stabilised HDPE netting, selected by hail risk and light requirement Hail, birds, sunburn and wind rub Needs stronger support than simple bird netting. Colour affects light, fruit colour and microclimate.
Insect net Fruit fly, moth, bug or bee exclusion Small mesh selected to target pest size Fruit fly, moths, bugs, beetles and other pests Tighter mesh can reduce airflow and increase humidity. Sealed joins are critical.
Shade net Hot inland fruit regions Shade percentage selected by crop and region Sunburn, heat stress and some wind Too much shade can reduce colour development or delay maturity.
Rain cover film Cherries, berries, grapes and premium fruit Clear UV anti-drip film or reinforced rain-cover film Rain cracking, rainfall, wet harvest conditions Acts like a sail. Needs smaller bays, strong hold-downs and operating rules.
Drape net Single-row orchard or vineyard protection Net draped over row/canopy Birds, sunburn, some hail and some insects Lower structure cost but more crop contact and manual handling.
Protection matrix

Best system by risk.

Each threat needs a different balance of airflow, strength, light transmission, sealing and seasonal operation.

🐦

Birds & bats

Use bird-safe exclusion netting. Best suited to permanent canopy, modular bay, or single-row drape systems.

🧊

Hail

Use hail-rated HDPE net and stronger support. Relevant to apples, pears, cherries, stone fruit and citrus.

☀️

Sunburn & heat

Use shade-rated or coloured nets carefully. Good for hot areas, but shading must not harm fruit colour or maturity.

💨

Wind

Use perimeter protection, windbreak mesh and balanced cable layouts. Wind load drives post and screw sizing.

🐛

Insects

Use smaller mesh and sealed edges. Useful for fruit fly, moths and other crop-specific pest pressure.

🌧

Rain & cracking

Use rain covers for cherries, berries and grapes. Smaller bays are safer than giant continuous film spans.

Crop-specific design

Different fruit. Different protection priorities.

Australia’s fruit regions vary from cool Tasmanian cherries to hot inland citrus and table grapes. The system should be matched to crop and local risk.

Cherries

High-value crop with strong need for bird, rain cracking, hail and wind protection. Rain cover and seasonal net operation can be major value drivers.

Apples & pears

Often suited to bird/hail net systems. Priorities include hail, birds, flying foxes, sunburn and packout improvement.

Citrus

Protection can target hail, wind rub, sunburn, birds, bats and sometimes bee exclusion for seedless varieties.

Berries

Bird pressure, hail, sun, rain and microclimate management are key. Tunnel-style or modular bay systems may suit some blocks.

Stone fruit

Peaches, nectarines, apricots and plums need protection from hail, birds, sunburn and rain-related quality loss.

Table grapes

Protection can target birds, sunburn, hail, rain splitting and bunch quality. Drape and side-net systems may suit vineyards.

Turnkey process

From concept to installed and operational.

Verdasheild is planned as a full turnkey system: site review, layout, engineering, supply, install and operating handover. Early projects should be treated as pilot installations while the system is refined.

Site assessment

Review crop type, hectares, row spacing, wind exposure, soil, machinery access and protection goals.

System selection

Choose bird net, hail net, rain cover, insect exclusion, shade or a combined system.

Engineering and layout

Confirm post spacing, cable layout, screw/pile selection, bracing and operating limits.

Install and handover

Install the structure, tension the netting system, test operation and train the grower or farm team.
