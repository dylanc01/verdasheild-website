Systems

Protection needs to match the crop, the site and the risk.

A bird net is not the same as a hail net. Rain cover behaves differently again. Arcovra’s system approach is built around selecting the right cover, spacing, support structure and seasonal operating method for the orchard.

Pilot installations

Early grower pilots, not overpromised systems.

Arcovra is currently at the development and pilot stage. The goal is to work with selected growers on practical trial installations, collect real operating data, and refine the system before larger commercial rollout.

  • Start with manageable orchard blocks
  • Test installation time and seasonal operation
  • Compare against existing timber or conventional net systems
  • Refine structure, netting, anchors and operating procedure
What this means for growers

A pilot installation should be treated as a collaborative development project. The system will be designed carefully, but final commercial claims should only be made after testing, engineering review and real-world performance data.


This keeps the approach honest: practical protection first, proof before scale, and no unrealistic promises before the system has been validated.

Netting guide

Core cover types and where they fit.

The tighter or more solid the cover, the higher the wind load. Bird netting can often work with wider spans. Rain film and fine insect mesh need more careful design because they catch more wind and can alter the orchard microclimate.

Cover type Main purpose Typical use Key design issue Arcovra design approach
Bird net Bird and bat exclusion Cherries, berries, apples, pears, grapes and stone fruit Wildlife safety, airflow, access and seasonal labour Wide-span or modular systems designed around faster handling and practical orchard access.
Hail net Impact protection Apples, pears, cherries, citrus and stone fruit Higher structural loading and light-management trade-offs Stronger net/support combinations where hail risk justifies the cost and structure.
Insect net Pest exclusion Fruit fly, moth, bug or beetle pressure Fine mesh reduces airflow and increases sealing requirements Used selectively where pest pressure justifies tighter mesh and more detailed edge control.
Shade net Sunburn and heat reduction Hot regions and exposed blocks Too much shade can affect colour, maturity and crop response Selected carefully by crop and region, not applied as a one-size-fits-all cover.
Rain cover film Rain cracking and wet harvest protection Cherries, berries, grapes and premium fruit Acts like a sail and needs smaller bays or controlled release Approached as a specific rain-cover option, not simply added to a bird-net structure.
Protection matrix

What are you protecting against?

The best system depends on the main risk. A cherry block exposed to rain cracking does not need the same structure as a citrus block fighting wind rub or an apple orchard needing hail protection.

🐦

Birds & bats

Best handled with exclusion netting that is installed, tensioned and maintained properly. Seasonal operation is often the biggest labour issue.

🧊

Hail

Requires stronger cover selection and support design. Hail protection is usually a higher-cost system but can protect an entire season’s crop value.

🐛

Insects

Fine mesh can help exclude pests, but it must be balanced with airflow, pollination, heat and access requirements.

🌧

Rain

Rain covers are especially relevant to cherries and other premium crops, but they introduce higher wind loads and need smaller, controlled sections.

☀️

Sunburn & heat

Shade can reduce fruit damage and stress, but the shade percentage and colour need to be matched to the crop and climate.

💨

Wind

Wind affects structure loads, net movement, fruit rub and anchor design. It should be part of the layout from the beginning.

Crops

Fruit-specific protection priorities.

Arcovra is initially focused on orchard and fruit protection systems. Each crop has different risks, economics and handling requirements.

Cherries

High-value crop with strong bird pressure, rain cracking risk, hail exposure and seasonal labour demand. A good first market for pilot systems.

Apples & pears

Often suited to bird and hail systems. Main drivers can include hail damage, sunburn, birds, flying foxes and packout improvement.

Citrus

Protection can focus on hail, wind rub, sunburn, birds and bats. Windbreak and perimeter protection can be just as important as overhead cover.

Berries

Bird pressure, hail, heat, rain and microclimate management matter. Smaller modular systems may be more suitable than large-span orchard layouts.

Stone fruit

Peaches, nectarines, apricots and plums may need protection from birds, hail, sunburn and rain-related quality loss.

Table grapes

Protection can target birds, sunburn, hail, rain splitting and bunch quality. Vineyard layouts may need a different format from orchard rows.

Turnkey pathway

From site review to practical installation.

The business model is intended to be full turnkey: concept layout, supply, installation management and handover. Early jobs should stay small enough to measure, refine and improve.

Site assessment

Review crop type, hectares, row spacing, wind exposure, soil, machinery access and existing netting problems.

System selection

Select the likely cover type: bird net, hail net, insect net, weather cover, or a staged combination.

Layout and review

Confirm proposed spacing, post arrangement, cable paths, anchors, access, operating limits and pilot scope.

Install and measure

Install the pilot, measure labour, observe operation, record issues and improve the system before scaling.
