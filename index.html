Systems

Different threats need different cover systems.

The most important design choice is not simply “what net?”, but what risk the grower is trying to control. Bird netting, hail netting, insect exclusion mesh and rain-cover film all impose different demands on the structure.

Bird Net System

Open mesh physical exclusion for birds and bats, with airflow and seasonal operation kept central to the layout.

Hail Net System

Stronger netting and support layouts for hail-prone crops where crop value justifies higher structure cost.

Insect Exclusion

Finer mesh systems for targeted pest exclusion where edge sealing and microclimate control become more important.

Rain Cover Option

Rain-sensitive crops need smaller bays, venting or controlled operation because film behaves very differently to porous net.

Research-led design rules

The cover changes the structure.

Orchard netting is not one category. Porous bird nets, hail nets, insect mesh and rain film all change wind load, airflow, light, heat, humidity, irrigation, spraying and labour.

  • Wider spans suit porous bird netting better than rain film.
  • Hail protection needs stronger structure and light-management planning.
  • Fine insect mesh can reduce airflow and increase heat/humidity risk.
  • Rain covers help keep fruit dry but need careful wind and bay-size control.
  • The operating system matters as much as the material.
Netting selection

Cover type comparison.

The tighter or more solid the cover, the more carefully the structure must be designed for wind, heat and handling.

Cover type Primary use Best suited crops Key risks controlled Design cautions
Bird net Physical exclusion of birds and bats Cherries, berries, apples, pears, grapes, stone fruit Fruit loss, pecking, bat damage, wildlife entry Wildlife-safe mesh, access, repairs, seasonal handling and edge closure.
Hail net Impact reduction and crop quality protection Apples, pears, cherries, stone fruit, citrus Hail impact, some sunburn, birds, wind rub More structure required; colour and density influence light and fruit response.
Insect net Targeted pest exclusion Stone fruit, citrus, berries, cherries, organic systems Fruit fly, moths, beetles, wasps and other pests Fine mesh can change airflow, humidity, pollination access and spray behaviour.

Crop applications

Protection priorities by fruit type.

The best system changes by crop. Arcovra’s first focus should stay on orchard protection where the value of protected fruit and labour savings can justify the structure.

Cherries

High-value crop with strong need for bird, rain cracking, hail and wind protection.

  • Bird netting
  • Rain cover over premium blocks
  • Seasonal handling focus

Apples & pears

Often driven by hail, birds, sunburn and packout improvement.

  • Hail netting
  • Bird exclusion
  • Light and vigour management

Citrus

Wind rub, hail, sunburn, bats and birds can all affect fruit quality.

  • Windbreak/perimeter protection
  • Hail net where justified
  • Sunburn management

Berries

Bird pressure, hail, rain and microclimate are major design factors.

  • Bird netting
  • Hail/rain protection
  • Smaller bay layouts

Stone fruit

Can need protection from birds, fruit fly, hail, sunburn and rain-related quality loss.

  • Bird netting
  • Insect exclusion
  • Hail or rain cover where needed

Table grapes

Protection can target birds, sunburn, hail and rain splitting.

  • Drape or side-net systems
  • Rain cover for premium fruit
  • Sun and bird control
Pilot installations

Test small. Measure everything. Scale after proof.

Arcovra is positioned as a pilot-stage orchard protection system. Early installations should stay controlled and measurable before broad commercial rollout.

Pilot projects should measure installation time, post/anchor behaviour, wind response, seasonal net operation, maintenance issues, grower feedback and cost compared with conventional timber or netting systems.


The goal is proof through real orchard performance rather than premature claims.

Australian fruit regions

Start with high-value orchard pilots.

The most logical early fit is high-value fruit where protection directly affects packout, marketability and seasonal labour: cherries first, then selected apple, pear, berry, stone fruit, citrus and table grape applications.

Australia-wide expansion should happen only after proving the system in real orchard blocks and adapting it to wind, soil, crop and operational requirements.
